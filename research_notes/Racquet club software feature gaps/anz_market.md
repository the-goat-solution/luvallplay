# ANZ Racquet Club Software Market: Competitors, National Body Systems, Market Size, Payments/Compliance, Pain Points

Research date: 17 Nov 2026. Method note: the session's network blocked direct page fetches of tennis.kiwi and helloclub.com (DNS refused), so most findings come from search-result extracts of primary pages (vendor, NSO and regional association sites, annual reports, news). Figures should be spot-checked against the live pages before publication. Several vendors named in the brief (Clubhub, Stack Team App, MyClubhouse, Swish-type tools, SportsGround as a separate brand, Revolutionise) returned little or no usable information. See Gaps.

## Q1. Competitor profiles (features, pricing, target clubs, sentiment)

### Takeaway
The NZ racquet market is effectively split between **Hello Club** (NZ-built, paid SaaS, endorsed by Pickleball NZ and widely used by badminton/squash clubs) and **national-body-funded platforms**: Tennis NZ funds **ClubSpark** for clubs and has now also approved **Sporty** (freemium, NZ-based). In Australia, Tennis Australia gives ClubSpark ("Book a Court" / play.tennis.com.au) free to affiliated clubs, and team sports use **PlayHQ** and **GameDay** (per-registration fees). Pickleball and padel in AU are going to commercial venue platforms (Playbypoint, Picklr's own systems, Playtomic). A white-label product competes mainly with Hello Club on price/features and with "free because the NSO pays" on tennis.

PNZA's chair framed it as needing systems that "scale alongside the sport's growth". — [Hello Club blog: PNZA partners with Hello Club](https://helloclub.com/blog/pnza-partners-with-hello-club) (date not confirmed; the page could not be fetched)

### Inferences
- Hello Club is the most direct competitor for a Christchurch-built white-label product. It is NZ-made, priced in NZD, supports POLi, has NSO endorsement in pickleball, and has penetration in badminton (WNBA) and squash. Its tiering is based on member counts, so per-member growth pushes clubs up tiers, which is a possible pricing wedge.
- In tennis, competing on price is hard because ClubSpark (and now Sporty) is free or NSO-funded. The opening is multi-sport venues (tennis + pickleball + padel), clubs unhappy with ClubSpark UX, or features ClubSpark lacks (social-night rotation, ladder/box leagues).
- Sporty's "free, ad-supported" model sets a price anchor of $0 for basic NZ club websites and registration. A paid product has to justify itself on time saved and on booking or payments features.
- AU team-sport platforms (PlayHQ, GameDay) monetise per registration or transaction and are built for team sports, not court booking. They are weak competitors for social racquet clubs but strong where an NSO mandates them.
- Commercial pickleball and padel venues are being captured by global, venue-oriented platforms (Playbypoint, Playtomic, PodPlay), leaving volunteer-run community clubs underserved.

### Gaps
- No usable information found on Clubhub, Stack Team App, MyClubhouse, or "Swish"-type local tools. SportsGround appears to be the corporate name behind Sporty (Sportsground Ltd), not a separate product, but this was not confirmed.
- No AUD pricing found for Hello Club, Friendly Manager, TeamApp or GameDay.
- Club sentiment: no forum or Facebook commentary was accessible. No reliable review-level sentiment was gathered for ClubSpark vs Hello Club vs Sporty (Capterra/GetApp review contents were not retrieved).
- The third Tennis NZ-approved platform (the page reportedly lists three) was not identified.
- Playtomic's ANZ footprint and pricing were not retrieved.

## Q2. What national/regional bodies use and mandate (badminton, table tennis, tennis, pickleball)

### Takeaway
Only tennis has a clear national system strategy in NZ: Tennis NZ funds ClubSpark and approves Sporty, framed around a "systemwide membership model" and Incorporated Societies Act 2022 compliance. Pickleball NZ has chosen Hello Club for affiliated-club registration. Badminton NZ had no national membership database and has had a national membership-management system on its priority list since about 2022, but its status was unconfirmed as of the 2024–25 reports. Regional badminton associations set their own affiliation rules (minimum registered players), and some use Hello Club. Table Tennis NZ runs its own Individual Membership Registration (IMR) numbers and ranking system. A third-party product will usually have to **coexist** with, and export to, NSO systems rather than replace them.

### Cited Findings
- **Tennis NZ**: moving to a "systemwide membership model" to improve data accuracy and club efficiency. Approved systems help clubs meet Incorporated Societies Act 2022 compliance. ClubSpark is preferred and fully funded, Sporty is newly approved, and clubs are told to "explore the available options and choose the platform that best meets their needs" (i.e. not a hard mandate, but a short approved list). — [Tennis NZ online membership hub](https://tennis.kiwi/clubs/online/); [staging copy](https://tennis-kiwi.amplifierpreview.co.nz/clubs/online/)
- **Tennis Australia**: ClubSpark is free to affiliated clubs; Book a Court / play.tennis.com.au is the national booking front-end. — [Tennis Australia: Using ClubSpark](https://tennis.com.au/clubs-coaches-officials/apps/using-clubspark)
- **Badminton NZ**: the Strengthen & Adapt initiative proposed a nationwide member/sport management platform because "the sport does not maintain national or regional membership databases". It targeted 80% of associations and their clubs implementing it by 2025, with $360,000 of funding over three years and post-2025 costs possibly shared with associations and clubs. — [BNZ Strengthen & Adapt summary (2022)](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Badminton-New-Zealand-Strengthen-Adapt-Initiative-1-Summary.pdf)
- BNZ's 2024 Annual Report says progress depends on "defining our future membership model and finalising our software requirements". It lists a membership (affiliation) model and a membership management system as 2025 priorities, which implies the system was **not yet in place** by 2024. The 2025 report notes "a year of significant transition" including the departure of CEO Stephen Nelson. — [BNZ 2024 Annual Report](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2024-Annual-Report.pdf); [BNZ Annual Report 2025](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Annual-Report-2025.pdf)
- **Regional badminton affiliation rules**: Badminton North Harbour requires at least 20 players registered to BNH, participation in BNH interclub, and registration forms by 30 March. Wellington North requires at least 12 registered players and reviews affiliation annually. — [BNH Registered Clubs Policy](https://www.bnh.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Registered-Clubs-Policy-1.pdf); [WNBA affiliated club policy (2021)](https://www.wnba.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/20211016-affliated_club_policy-FINALv3.pdf)
- WNBA runs membership, court booking, classes and teams-league entries via Hello Club. — [WNBA memberships](https://www.wnba.org.nz/memberships/)
- **Badminton Canterbury**: no current info retrieved on its registration or interclub system. Recent tournament notices list office@badmintoncanterbury.com / badmintoncanterbury.com. — [2023 Canterbury Masters notice](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2023-Canterbury-Masters.pdf)
- **Badminton Australia**: registration runs via state associations and clubs. A BWF ID requires membership of a club affiliated to a state/territory association. Badminton NT provisionally accepts registrations pending payment and approval. Badminton WA appears on playsport.com, and at least one AU association (FDBA) uses PlayHQ for domestic competition. Badminton Australia has advertised a "Database & Member Services Manager" role. — [BWF ID form](https://form.jotform.com/242390868849070); [Badminton WA on playsport](https://playsport.com/BadmintonWA); [FDBA PlayHQ](https://fdba.com.au/domestic-competitions/playhq/); [JumpinSport job ad](https://jumpinsport.com/database-member-services-manager-badminton-australia-melbourne-sydney-or-canberra-170988)
- **Table Tennis NZ**: issues Individual Membership Registration (IMR) numbers. TTNZ ranking lists are the official source for seedings and draws at sanctioned tournaments, modelled on the ITTF system. New players get a provisional rating after their first tournament. TTNZ is made up of affiliated regional associations. — [TTNZ Apply for IMR](https://tabletennis.org.nz/how/apply-imr-number); [TTNZ Ranking System Overview](https://tabletennis.org.nz/rankings/ranking-system-regulations); [TTNZ affiliated associations](https://tabletennis.org.nz/About/ttnz-affiliated-associations/Associations)
- **Pickleball NZ (PNZA)**: accredited by Sport NZ as the national governing body on 27 Nov 2025. Partnered with Hello Club for affiliated-club registrations, renewals and payments. Club/playing-group affiliation is defined on the PNZA site. — [PNZA strategic plan](https://www.pnza.org.nz/pnza/pnza-strategic-plan); [PNZA club affiliation](https://www.pnza.org.nz/membership/pnza-clubplaying-group-affiliation); [Hello Club blog](https://helloclub.com/blog/pnza-partners-with-hello-club)
- **Pickleball Australia**: has its own membership portal and a DUPR partnership for ratings. No national court-booking platform was found. — [Pickleball Australia membership](https://www.pickleballaus.org/membership); [DUPR](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-and-pickleball-australia)
- **Squash NZ**: no system mandate found; clubs use a mix of tools. — [Squash NZ where to play](https://squashnz.co.nz/where-to-play)

### Inferences
- For a Christchurch badminton-first product, the badminton NZ system vacuum is the key opportunity and risk. If BNZ picks a national platform (Hello Club is a plausible candidate given PNZA and WNBA usage, but that is speculation), clubs may be pushed onto it. Talking to BNZ and Badminton Canterbury before building is advisable.
- Integration needs: export or sync of member lists to the regional association (for minimum-registered-player counts and affiliation fees), TTNZ IMR numbers for table tennis, DUPR IDs and ratings for pickleball, and BWF IDs for badminton tournament players. Rankings and tournament seeding stay with the NSOs, so a club product should consume them rather than compete with them.
- Tennis clubs on ClubSpark are effectively locked in by NSO funding plus data flows. A third-party product would need ClubSpark coexistence (e.g. social-night tools alongside it) rather than replacement.

### Gaps
- Whether BNZ launched a national membership system in 2025–26, and which vendor, is unconfirmed (the annual report body was not retrievable).
- Badminton Canterbury's current registration, affiliation fees and interclub tooling are unknown.
- Affiliation fee amounts (per player or per club) for BNZ, TTNZ, PNZA and Tennis NZ were not found.
- Badminton Australia's national registration platform (if any) is unconfirmed.
- Whether any NSO publishes an API for third-party integration was not found.

## Q3. Market size: clubs, members and growth per sport (NZ & AU)

### Takeaway
Hard national counts are patchy. Tennis NZ had about 41k club members in 2022/23, and Tennis Auckland about 12.7k in 2024/25. Pickleball Australia has about 27–28k members and about 410–425 affiliated clubs/venues (mid-2026). NZ pickleball is booming regionally but has no national total found. AU padel courts grew 50%+ in 12 months to April 2026, from a small base (about 30 clubs per one interview).

### Cited Findings
- Tennis NZ: membership up 14% to 41,238; 159 clubs on ClubSpark (2022/23; older data). — [Tennis NZ Annual Report 2023](https://tennis.kiwi/assets/Files-pdf-word-docs-etc/Tennis-NZ-Annual-Report-2023-LR.pdf)
- Tennis NZ 2024/25: casual participation up 6.3%, with 33,663 players at clubs, schools and venues. The national club-member total was not retrieved. — [Tennis NZ 2025 Annual Report](https://tennis.kiwi/assets/Tennis-New-Zealand-2025-Annual-Report-online.pdf?vid=5)
- Tennis Auckland 2024/25: club membership up almost 3% to 12,749, including 1,001 new members from a "Love Tennis" weekend. — [Tennis Auckland annual report 2024/25](https://www.tennisauckland.co.nz/27303-tennis-auckland-annual-report-2024-25-final-website-sponsors)
- Tennis Canterbury reports club membership growth of 10% (year unclear). — [Tennis Canterbury report](https://www.tenniscanterbury.org/_files/ugd/88cd1c_915a63bb48934afa95590708ae21e01d.pdf)
- Tennis Australia has eight Member Associations. Its 2024–25 Annual Report has a participation section, but the figures were not retrieved. — [TA Annual Report 2024–25](https://www.tennis.com.au/content/dam/tennisaustralia/documents/reports/annual-reports/tennis-australia-annual-report-2024-25.pdf)
- Pickleball Australia: "more than 27,000 members and over 410 affiliated clubs and venues" (DUPR, June 2026). Wikipedia lists 27,974 registered players and 425 clubs as of 29 Jun 2026. Older PA pages cite 19,500 members, and a 2025 release gives NSW 6,093 and QLD 5,172 members. — [DUPR & Pickleball Australia](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-and-pickleball-australia); [Wikipedia: Pickleball in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball_in_Australia); [PA media talking points](https://www.pickleballaus.org/media-talking-points)

### Inferences
- In AU, about 410–425 pickleball clubs and venues and fast-growing padel form a sizeable new-club segment with no NSO-funded club platform (unlike tennis). That makes them the most open segment for a white-label product.
- Many NZ pickleball groups operate in tennis clubs (shared courts), and ClubSpark already books pickleball at some. Multi-sport court booking is table stakes.

## Q4. Payment norms, GST invoicing, privacy law

### Takeaway
NZ clubs commonly use Stripe for cards and POLi for account-to-account payments. POLi still operates in NZ (local ownership via Merco) but closed in Australia in Sept 2023. Bank transfer and cash remain common, though this is not directly evidenced in sources found. Under NZ GST, supplies of $200 or less need no taxable supply information. Above $200, information must be supplied on request, and from $200 to $1,000 that includes the GST number and GST amount. NZ's Privacy Act gained IPP3A (indirect collection notification) from 1 May 2026. Australia's small-business (under A$3m turnover) exemption still covers many NFP clubs, but its removal is proposed and unlegislated.

### Cited Findings
- POLi AU closed at the end of Sept 2023; NZ continues under local distributor Merco. — [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/australia-post-to-close-poli-payments-597950); [Western Weekender](https://westernweekender.com.au/?p=53741); [GameDay notice](https://community.mygameday.app/2023/07/customer-notification-poli-payments-for-australian-customers-to-cease/)
- POLi NZ status page mentions "reallocating resources to other product initiatives", says Classic POLi is unaffected, and offers "Pay by Bank" (open banking) as a method. — [POLi NZ status](https://status.polipay.co.nz/incidents/xgvk2p2j5pcn)
- Consumer NZ warns that POLi may void bank protections (a consumer-trust concern). — [Consumer NZ](https://consumer.org.nz/articles/consumer-nz-unpacks-the-popular-payment-method-that-could-void-your-bank-protection)
- Hello Club POLi fee: 1%, capped at $1.00 excl. GST for non-profits. Sporty (2021): POLi 1% + GST, Stripe 2.9% + 30c. — [Hello Club help](https://help.helloclub.com/en/articles/2514965-account-to-account-payments-in-new-zealand); [Scoop 2021](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/BU2110/S00369.htm)
- AU per-registration "service fee" norms: Football Australia 1.98% (2026), PlayHQ 1.99% plus a merchant fee, and Netball NSW per-player transaction fees. — [Football Australia FAQ](https://footballaustralia.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/2026%20Service%20Fee%20FAQs.pdf); [PlayHQ pricing](https://get.playhq.com/pricing); [Netball NSW 2026 fees](https://nsw.netball.com.au/sites/nsw/files/2025-11/2026%20Fees_0.pdf)
- NZ GST taxable supply information (TSI) rules (from 1 Apr 2023): no TSI is required for supplies of $200 or less (but GST-registered suppliers must keep records). Above $200, TSI must be provided within 28 days of a request. From $200 to $1,000 it must include the GST number and GST amount. — [Deloitte NZ](https://www.deloitte.com/nz/en/services/tax/perspectives/leveraging-the-new-taxable-supply-information-requirements.html); [PwC NZ](https://www.pwc.co.nz/services/tax/new-gst-invoicing-and-record-keeping-rules.html); [Stripe NZ guide](https://stripe.com/nz/resources/more/writing-invoices-in-new-zealand)
- NZ Privacy Act 2020: the Privacy Amendment Act 2025 added IPP3A (notification when collecting personal information indirectly), in force 1 May 2026 and applying to information collected from that date. Cloud providers acting purely on an agency's behalf do not trigger IPP3A, and the club remains responsible under s11. — [Bell Gully](https://www.bellgully.com/insights/preparing-for-ipp-3a-new-requirements-effective-1-may-2026/); [Privacy Commissioner IPP3A](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ipp3a/); [Ministry of Justice](https://www.justice.govt.nz/about/news-and-media/news/new-privacy-information-principle-in/); [Wynn Williams](https://wynnwilliams.co.nz/insights/general-business-smes/ipp3a-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Australia: NFPs with turnover under A$3m may fall under the small-business exemption. Removal is proposed (agreed in principle) but not legislated as of the latest authoritative sources. Some 2026 blogs claim it is gone, which conflicts with government-focused sources. — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/de-de/wissen/publications/dc6752ce/does-the-privacy-act-apply-to-your-nfp-the-answer-might-surprise-you); [Canberra Cyber Hub](https://canberracyberhub.com.au/news-and-events/update-privacy-reforms-what-it-means-cyber-government-and-small-business); [Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/government/9614-small-businesses-will-be-no-longer-exempt-from-the-privacy-act)
- Incorporated Societies Act 2022 (NZ): NZASA says involuntary dissolutions mostly happen when a committee cannot meet its compliance obligations under the Act. Tennis NZ positions approved systems as aiding compliance. — [NZASA](https://www.asa.org.nz/news/media-releases/government-committee-suggests-investigation); [Tennis NZ](https://tennis.kiwi/clubs/online/)

### Inferences
- An NZ product should offer Stripe cards, an A2A option (POLi or open-banking "Pay by Bank"), and bank-transfer reconciliation (e.g. via Xero, which Sporty integrates with). It should also offer GST-aware receipts that switch on GST number and amount when a club is GST-registered and a supply exceeds $200.
- Most club memberships and court fees are under $200, so simple receipts suffice. Invoices for team or corporate bookings over $200 need full TSI.
- Privacy features that differentiate: IPP3A notices when importing members from an association or school list, data-export and deletion tools, and NZ/AU data residency messaging. An Incorporated Societies Act-compliant member register (consents, officer records) is a compliance selling point.

### Gaps
- Windcave, Afterpay and cash-handling norms for clubs were not researched. No evidence was found on prevalence of bank transfer vs card in clubs.
- The NZ cross-border disclosure principle (IPP12) applied to overseas SaaS hosting was not confirmed.
- How GST applies to club subscriptions specifically (e.g. whether many small clubs are below the $60k GST registration threshold) was not confirmed.

## Q5. Local pain points clubs voice

### Takeaway
The dominant NZ pain point is the volunteer crisis: fewer volunteers, heavier workload on one or two people, compliance burden (Incorporated Societies Act, child safety) and rising costs. Racquet-specific pain points (casual booking, court access, demand outstripping supply in pickleball) appear in club and news material. Direct club commentary on software was not accessible.

### Cited Findings
- The 2025 National Sport Club Survey (NZASA + AUT SPRINZ) found volunteer numbers improved slightly but remain well below pre-pandemic levels, with widespread concern about workload and burnout, and rising costs threatening affordability. — [Scoop (Oct 2025)](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/CU2510/S00114.htm); [NZASA](https://www.asa.org.nz/news/media-releases/rising-costs-threaten-affordability-of-community-sport-)

### Inferences
- The strongest positioning is "less volunteer admin": automated renewals and reminders, self-serve casual booking with smart-lock access, automated affiliation/registration exports to associations, Incorporated Societies Act register and AGM tooling, and handover-friendly setup for committee turnover.
- Social/club-night rotation (fair court allocation, mixing levels, box leagues), which matters to badminton and pickleball, is not a highlighted feature of ClubSpark, Sporty or PlayHQ in sources found. This is a probable gap but unverified against Hello Club's feature list.
