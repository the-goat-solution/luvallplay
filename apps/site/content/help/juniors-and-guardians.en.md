## The idea in one line

Juniors play. Adults manage the account.

On Luvallplay, a junior is anyone under 16. The plan is that juniors never have their own sign-in. A parent or guardian, signed in with their own adult account, adds and manages each child's profile.

This is planned, not live. It isn't available today, and details may change before clubs can sign up. We're sharing the plan so you know what to expect, and so you can tell us what your club needs.

## What a guardian will be able to do

- Add a child's profile. It holds a name and a birth year. It has no email address and no phone number.
- Join the child to a club.
- Sign the child up for sessions, and pay if the club charges.
- Sign consent forms and waivers.
- Record emergency contacts and medical notes.
- See the child's attendance.
- Withdraw the child, or have the child's details deleted.

One guardian can manage several children. One child can have 2 guardians.

## What juniors won't have

Some parts of Luvallplay are for adults only. Junior profiles are planned to have none of these:

- Direct messages
- Social posts or follows
- Marketplace access
- Public photos
- A place on a public leaderboard, unless the guardian chooses to opt in. Even then, only a first name and an initial are shown.
- Vision footage, unless the guardian agrees

## What clubs will do

Junior tools are planned as a module your club switches on. See [modules: pick what you need](/help/modules-pick-what-you-need). With it on:

- You get a junior roster with guardian and emergency contacts. Only the people who need it can see it.
- Medical notes are limited to the roles you name.
- Coaches and volunteers who work with juniors need a recorded safety check before they're assigned to juniors.
- Your club confirms it has a junior safeguarding policy when it switches the module on.
- Messages about a junior always go to the guardian. Coaches can't message children directly.

## Turning 16

At 16, a guardian will be able to invite the young person to take over their own account, if the young person agrees. Their history comes with them.

## Safeguarding basics for any club

Software doesn't keep children safe. People and habits do. These are general good practice, not legal advice:

- **Have a written policy.** Keep it short enough that people read it.
- **Name a contact person.** Someone parents and juniors can go to with a worry, and who knows what to do next.
- **Check your people.** Follow the safety-checking rules that apply in your country for adults who work with children.
- **Avoid one-to-one.** No adult alone with a child out of sight. That covers changing rooms, car rides and private messages.
- **Talk to the guardian.** Schedule changes, feedback and photos go through the parent or guardian.
- **Ask before you photograph.** Get the guardian's permission before taking or sharing any photo of a junior.
- **Know who's collecting.** Be clear on when the session ends and who each child goes home with.
- **Keep emergency contacts to hand** at every session.

Your sport's national body probably publishes a safeguarding guide and a template policy. Start there. The law differs between countries, so check the rules where your club plays.

## A note on other people's children's details

A child's details need extra care. Collect only what you need, show it only to the people who need it, and delete it when the child leaves the club.

If your club keeps junior details in a spreadsheet today, this is a good moment to check who can open it.
