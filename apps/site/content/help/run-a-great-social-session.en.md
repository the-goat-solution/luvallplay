## What makes a good session?

People come back when 3 things happen: they get plenty of games, the games are fun at their level, and someone noticed they were there. None of that needs software. It needs a bit of structure and one person paying attention.

These tips work for any sport with courts, fields or tables. Take what suits your club.

## Before the session

- **Know your numbers.** Count your courts or fields, and how many players each one fits. Add a few for people resting. That's your capacity. Past it, everyone waits too long.
- **Take sign-ups.** Even a rough headcount beats a surprise. A waitlist is kinder than a packed hall.
- **Arrive early.** Nets up, lights on, equipment out before the start time. The first 10 minutes set the mood.
- **Name a host.** One person whose job tonight is the session, not their own game.

## The first 10 minutes

- Greet people by name as they arrive. Yes, every time.
- Tick off who's here. It tells you who's missing and who's new.
- Get the first games going fast. Don't wait for stragglers.
- Say the plan out loud: how rotation works tonight, when you finish, where the water is.

## Rotation that feels fair

Most grumbles at social sessions are about waiting. Pick one system, explain it, and stick to it.

- **A visible queue.** Names on a whiteboard, pegs on a board or a screen. If people can see the order, they trust it.
- **Fixed-length games.** One game, or a set time on the clock. Then everyone comes off.
- **Winners don't stay on all night.** Winners staying on is fun for the strongest players and nobody else. If you use it, cap it at 2 games.
- **Mix the partners.** In doubles and team sports, split pairs and teams as people come off. New combinations every game.
- **Watch the bench.** If someone has sat out 2 rounds, they go on next. No debate.

## Mixing levels

A mixed room is the hardest part and the best part.

- **Balance each game.** Pair a stronger player with a newer one, on both sides.
- **Run a level court some of the time.** Give your strongest players one hard game so they aren't holding back all night. Then mix them back in.
- **Give beginners a soft start.** Put new players on with your most patient members first. Patient, not best.
- **Coach by invitation only.** Unasked advice in the middle of a game puts people off. A tip after the game, if they want it, is welcome.

## Welcoming newcomers

A first visit decides whether there's a second.

1. Meet them at the door. Learn their name and use it.
2. Show them around: bags, toilets, water, spare equipment.
3. Explain the rotation in 2 sentences.
4. Introduce them to 2 people by name.
5. Get them into a game within 10 minutes.
6. Find them before they leave. Ask how it went, and tell them when you play next.

## Safety and comfort

- Start with a few minutes of warm-up. Cold starts cause injuries.
- Keep bags, bottles and spare equipment off the playing area.
- Know where the first aid kit is, and who has a phone.
- Wipe up spills and sweat on the floor straight away.
- If juniors play at your club, read [juniors and guardians](/help/juniors-and-guardians).

## Finishing well

- Call the last game 15 minutes before the end, so nobody is cut off mid-rally.
- Pack down together. Many hands, and it's when people chat.
- Say when the next session is before people scatter.
- Thank your helpers by name.

## Afterwards

Note who came, who was new and who you haven't seen for a while. A short "missed you last week" message brings more people back than any poster.
