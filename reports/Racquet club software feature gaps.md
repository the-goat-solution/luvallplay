# Sell the club night before the court grid

Three Wolves already does the hardest part of running a social racquet club better than almost anyone: the live court-rotation board, the Arena points-and-ranks game, attendance-driven streaks, member self-service on Stripe, and an NZ not-for-profit finance module with bank reconciliation and an AGM report. What it lacks is the plumbing that turns one club's app into a product many clubs can buy: a tenant model, configurable products and schedules, named staff logins with roles, club-owned payments (Stripe Connect), and an in-app content editor to replace per-club Sanity. Beyond that it lacks the commercial features rival platforms treat as standard: court-by-court time-slot booking, packs and guest passes, waitlist auto-promotion, push notifications, rating integrations, leagues and coaching. The winning move is not to copy CourtReserve or Playtomic. Instead, own a niche they have left behind: **session-based, volunteer-run racquet clubs** (badminton first, then table tennis and social pickleball), priced at or below Hello Club's NZ$58/month entry tier, with "no booking commission, your club owns its money and data" as the promise. Padel and commercial venues should wait for a later "venue tier", because they need a court-hour pricing engine Three Wolves does not have and they are being captured by well-funded incumbents. The roughly 4–7 months of platform rebuild is the real gating item, not features. So for the next 90 days, line up 3–5 design-partner clubs during the October–February committee-decision window while that rebuild happens, rather than adding more player features. One caveat runs through everything below: several vendor sites (CourtReserve, PlayByPoint, Playtomic, Hello Club, Stripe) were blocked during research. Most competitor prices therefore come from search summaries and third-party listings, and must be checked on the live pages before you quote them to anyone.

## Three Wolves leads on club-night operations, gamification and NZ finance

The open-play court board is the most commercially valuable thing in the codebase. Organisers drag players into tiers, the queue favours fewest games and then longest wait while avoiding repeat partners, it runs 1–4 courts on a venue TV and players' phones, and it concludes the day with a summary ([lib/casual-play.ts](../lib/casual-play.ts); [components/casual-play/CasualPlay.tsx](../components/casual-play/CasualPlay.tsx)). That matters because the research found rotation tooling is **fragmented across dozens of tiny apps and open-source PWAs** (Qcourt, PaQueueKa, NetRally, Shuttl, Racket Social, BBQueue), usually separate from the sign-up and payments app. Organisers therefore juggle two or three tools on a club night ([PaQueueKa](https://paqueueka.info/); [NetRally](https://netrally.io/); [BBQueue](https://github.com/darklight721/bbqueue)). The closest badminton-specific rival, UK-built ShuttleOps, combines a "digital peg board" with sessions, Stripe Direct Debit and club leagues, and reviewers praise exactly that peg-board fairness ([ShuttleOps App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/shuttleops/id6751508150)). Three Wolves already joins up the whole chain: recurring sessions with a cap, a waitlist, paid sign-up, a roster that becomes attendance automatically, the rotation board, results, and a leaderboard. The research found no vendor that leads with that end-to-end club-night loop. Visible fairness is also a proven "love driver": it takes the awkward "who plays next" decision off the organiser.

The second asset is engagement. Wolf Points reward attendance, round-robin wins and pair challenges, with bonuses, a 35-step rank ladder across four leagues, live "Alpha Wolf" seats, anti-farming caps and disputed-score handling ([lib/ranks.ts](../lib/ranks.ts); [lib/arena-db.ts](../lib/arena-db.ts)). Streaks are computed against the club's actual play days and excuse absences ([lib/stats-periods.ts](../lib/stats-periods.ts)). The social and pickleball apps offer leaderboards and win records ([PickleballOp](https://pickleballop.app/); [Reclub](https://pickleball.reclub.co/)), and Playo offers redeemable "karma points" ([Playo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.techmash.playo&hl=en_SG)). None of the club-management platforms researched showed anything as deep. This matters more now that growth has slowed: US pickleball grew only **1.3% in H1 2026** after five boom years, which pushes venues towards retention tooling ([SFIA mid-year](https://sfia.org/?p=10831)).

The third asset is the finance module: a Stripe-to-ledger import, bank reconciliation with learned rules, Akahu open-banking feeds, and a Statement of Receipts and Payments in the NZ Tier 4 not-for-profit format for the AGM ([lib/bank-match.ts](../lib/bank-match.ts); [app/admin/financials/report/page.tsx](../app/admin/financials/report/page.tsx)). This speaks directly to NZ's volunteer crisis. Club volunteers have fallen **28% since 2018** ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2025/08/03/grassroots-sports-clubs-doing-it-tough-report/)), and committees that cannot meet their Incorporated Societies Act 2022 obligations are the main cause of involuntary dissolutions ([NZASA](https://www.asa.org.nz/news/media-releases/government-committee-suggests-investigation)). A treasurer who can produce AGM accounts in one click is a buyer, and the research found no competitor that sells this. Keep all three assets, along with membership self-service built on Stripe subscription schedules ([lib/membership-self-service.ts](../lib/membership-self-service.ts)) and the player API, webhooks and MCP server ([app/api/mcp/route.ts](../app/api/mcp/route.ts)). Generalise them rather than rebuild them.

Four of the gaps matter more than the rest. The first is **court-by-court booking**: Three Wolves' only bookable unit is "a spot at the play day on date X", and the `sessions` table uses the date itself as the primary key, so the system can hold just one session per calendar day ([scripts/migrate.mjs](../scripts/migrate.mjs); [lib/db.ts](../lib/db.ts)). That is fine for a badminton club hiring hall time. It rules out padel and commercial tennis or pickleball, where revenue per court-hour is the core operating number ([Golfmanager](https://golfmanager.com/how-golf-clubs-are-adding-padel-and-other-sports/)). The second is **flexible ways to pay**: there are no multi-visit packs, guest passes, family accounts, promo codes, admin refund button or player-initiated cancellation, and cash is recorded by hand as an out-of-band Stripe invoice ([app/admin/members/page.tsx](../app/admin/members/page.tsx)). The third is **notifications**. Three Wolves sends email only, and its waitlist just notifies people; nobody is promoted automatically ([lib/waitlist-notify.ts](../lib/waitlist-notify.ts)). Missed waitlist notifications are a recurring complaint in Spond's reviews, so this is a trust issue, not a nicety ([Spond App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/spond/id755596884?see-all=reviews)). The fourth is **ratings and competitions**. Wolf Points measure activity and wins rather than skill, and there are no leagues, ladders, coaching or lessons. Pickleball clubs now treat DUPR as expected: 2026 DUPR integrations span club software, open-play apps and tournament tools ([DUPR–TeamReach](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-and-teamreach-announce-strategic-partnership-to-power-player-ratings-and-engagement-across-one-of-pickleballs-largest-communities); [DUPR–PickleQ](https://www.dupr.com/post/pickleq-streamlines-open-play-and-dupr-match-reporting)).

The bigger gap is underneath the features. There is **no club or tenant ID anywhere in roughly 50 tables**. "Three Wolves" appears in 53 source files, "Pacific/Auckland" is written literally in 16, and NZD in 37. Phone sign-in rejects non-+64 numbers. The membership model is typed as Friday or Wednesday only, and admin access is one shared password with no roles or audit log ([lib/constants.ts](../lib/constants.ts); [lib/phone.ts](../lib/phone.ts); [lib/admin-session.ts](../lib/admin-session.ts)). Everything is wired to one Stripe account, one Google Calendar, one Sanity dataset and one database. No paying club can sit on that, whatever features are added on top. (A small housekeeping point: the README describes a 14-day booking window while the code uses 30 days and a 5 PM cutoff. Tidy that before anyone else relies on the docs.)

## Own the session-based volunteer club, starting with badminton in Canterbury

The incumbents have moved away from small clubs. CourtReserve's legacy listing showed a US$25/month base plan. Today its tiers run **US$159–549/month**, gated by court count, and its branded app costs a US$499 setup fee plus a one-year contract ([Capterra legacy](https://www.capterra.com/p/152562/CourtReserve/pricing/); [Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/pickleball-gear/best-pickleball-court-reservation-software)). It raised **US$54M** in October 2025 to push into North America ([Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20251021345748/courtreserve-secures-a-54-million-growth-investment-from-mainsail-partners-to-accelerate-innovation-in-racquet-paddle-club-software)). Playtomic, with **6,000 clubs and €65M** of fresh funding, takes a commission on bookings and owns the player relationship. Clubs resent both ([Cluster Padel](https://www.clusterpadel.com/en/associate-news/20250319/playtomic-raises-%E2%82%AC65-million-to-strengthen-its-leadership-in-europe-and-accelerate-its-exp.aspx); [Padel iQ (competitor)](https://padeliq.io/compare/playtomic)). In Australia, PlayByPoint became the **exclusive platform for the National Pickleball League's venues** in March 2026 ([Ministry of Sport](https://ministryofsport.com/npl-australia-selects-playbypoint-as-exclusive-club-management-partner/)). Commercial venues are being captured, and volunteer clubs are being left behind.

Volunteer clubs that hire hall time for club nights sell sessions, not court-hours. UK badminton examples show the pattern: seasonal membership plus pay-and-play guests (£3 member, £11 non-member per session at one ClubSpark club), with membership capped by hall time ([Markland Hill](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/MarklandHillRacquetsClub/Badminton/Schedule); [Ely BC](https://sites.google.com/site/elyvictbad/the-club/membership-fees)). Table tennis club nights and pickleball open play have the same shape, and pickleball has the identical "who plays next on which court" problem that paddle stacks solve on paper ([Selkirk](https://www.selkirk.com/blogs/pickleball-education/pickleball-etiquette-how-the-paddle-rack-system-works-during-open-play)). The positioning is therefore **"the club-night app for session-based racquet clubs: sign-up, pay, rotate, score, rank, and do the books, in one place, with no booking commission."**

At home, the direct competitor is **Hello Club**, not CourtReserve. It is NZ-built, priced at NZ$58–298/month by member band, supports POLi account-to-account payments, is the chosen platform of Pickleball NZ, and runs membership, court booking and teams-league entries for the Wellington North Badminton Association ([Hello Club pricing](https://helloclub.com/pricing); [Hello Club–PNZA](https://helloclub.com/blog/pnza-partners-with-hello-club); [WNBA](https://www.wnba.org.nz/memberships/)). It is generic club software, and it charges extra for light and access control ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/omni/instead-of/hello-club/pricing)). Beat it on club-night depth and treasurer tooling, not on court booking. In tennis, ClubSpark is fully funded by Tennis NZ and free to affiliated clubs in Australia ([Tennis NZ](https://tennis.kiwi/clubs/online/); [Tennis Australia](https://tennis.com.au/clubs-coaches-officials/apps/using-clubspark)). Don't try to replace it. Sell the rotation board and Arena as a social-night add-on that runs alongside it.

Badminton has a vacuum that is both your biggest opportunity and your biggest risk. Badminton NZ admitted in 2022 that "the sport does not maintain national or regional membership databases" and earmarked **$360,000** for a national platform. Its 2024 report still listed the membership model and software requirements as unfinished priorities, and the 2025 report describes "a year of significant transition" ([BNZ Strengthen & Adapt](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Badminton-New-Zealand-Strengthen-Adapt-Initiative-1-Summary.pdf); [BNZ 2024 Annual Report](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2024-Annual-Report.pdf); [BNZ 2025 Annual Report](https://badminton.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Annual-Report-2025.pdf)). If BNZ picks a vendor, clubs will follow it. Whether BNZ has already chosen one was not confirmed, and you need to find out before writing much more code.

## Build the platform first, then the features clubs will pay for

The roadmap below separates what must exist before any club pays from what can follow. The platform items are not optional. Each one removes a hard blocker identified in the codebase. The effort figures come from the architecture researcher, assume one experienced developer, and are rough estimates rather than measurements ([multi-tenant notes](../research_notes/Racquet%20club%20software%20feature%20gaps/multi_tenant_architecture.md)).

| Platform change (must-have) | Plain-English meaning | Rough effort |
|---|---|---|
| Club (tenant) model in the database | Add a `clubs` table and a `club_id` to every table, make Three Wolves club #1, give sessions a proper ID instead of the date, and separate one global person login from per-club memberships. Postgres row-level security stops one club ever seeing another's data | 2–4 weeks |
| Club resolved from the web address | `wolves.yourproduct.nz` or the club's own domain loads that club's logo, colours, name and installable app icon. Vercel documents this pattern, with SSL issued automatically ([Vercel multi-tenant docs](https://vercel.com/docs/platforms/multi-tenant-platforms/proxy-and-routing)) | 2–3 weeks incl. custom domains |
| Configuration replaces hard-coding | Time zone, currency, phone country, prices, capacity rules, booking window and languages move from code into club settings. Remove the hard-coded mini-game roster of real players' first names | Folded into the above, but touches 50+ files |
| Internal scheduling engine | Venues, courts or halls, repeating session templates, and exceptions, replacing "any Google Calendar event is a play day". Google Calendar becomes an outbound feed only | 3–6 weeks |
| Club-owned payments via Stripe Connect | Each club connects its own Stripe account and money goes straight to the club. With Stripe-owned pricing and loss liability (new in the 2026-06-24 API) the platform pays no Connect fees and carries no chargeback risk ([Stripe SaaS platforms](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/saas-platforms-and-marketplaces); [Stripe changelog](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/dahlia/2026-06-24/express-connected-accounts-v2-stripe-owned-pricing-negative-balance-liability)). Three Wolves' own subscribers must be re-created on its connected account | 2–3 weeks |
| Named staff logins, roles, audit log | Owner, admin, coach and organiser roles replace the single shared password, every admin action is logged, and you get an audited "support login" as platform owner | 2–3 weeks |
| In-app content editor | Club news, pages and session descriptions live in the app, not Sanity. A Sanity dataset per club is unaffordable (reportedly about US$999/month per extra dataset, with seats capped at 50 on Growth) ([Sanity pricing](https://www.sanity.io/pricing)) | 2–3 weeks |
| Email and sign-in that scale | Send from a platform domain under the club's name with the club's reply-to address; make email codes or links (or passkeys) the default and SMS optional. NZ SMS costs about US$0.07–0.08 each, which would reach about US$16k/month at 1,000 clubs ([Google Identity Platform pricing](https://cloud.google.com/identity-platform/pricing)). Move off Resend's free tier and its 100 emails/day limit | ~1 week |
| Onboarding wizard and data export | Create club, subdomain, Stripe link, theme and invite admins; a CSV import of existing members; a one-click "export my club" | ~2 weeks |

That adds up to roughly **16–25 developer-weeks before the configuration clean-up**, so plan on 4–7 months. It is the honest reason "soon" means mid-2027 for a properly multi-tenant launch. A stopgap exists: copy the codebase once per club, with its own deployment, database and Stripe account. That could get one or two pilot clubs live faster, but every copy has to be maintained separately and migrated later. Only use it if a design partner will not wait, and make sure each pilot club opens its own Stripe account so it can be connected to the platform later.

The sequencing logic is that every row marked "before first paying club" is something a badminton, table tennis or social pickleball club would notice missing in its first month. Rows marked "venue tier" belong to a different customer. Padel needs the full stack (court grid, split payment at booking, level-gated open matches); open matches are "almost 20%" of the bookings Playtomic manages ([Padel Smash Academy podcast](https://share.transistor.fm/s/d3433ec5)). Building that before you have referenceable clubs would spread a solo founder too thin. Badminton has no DUPR equivalent, and no widely used social-level badminton rating turned up in the research ([BadmintonPeople](https://www.badmintonpeople.dk/Info/Features/Default.aspx)). Wolf Points could grow into that rating, and that would be a real differentiator.

For mobile, stay PWA-first. Each club's domain installs as its own branded app, and iOS supports web push for home-screen apps from 16.4 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/?p=16535)). Do not promise each club its own App Store listing. Apple guideline 4.2.6 rejects template apps unless the club itself submits them under its own developer account ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). If store presence matters later, ship one product-branded app with a club picker, and sell per-club native apps only as a paid add-on.

## Price at or below Hello Club and keep the payments margin small

Two price bands exist. US court-booking platforms charge **US$159–600/month**. NZ community-club tools charge **NZ$58–154/month** for most clubs, and free tools (Spond, TeamApp, Sporty) set a NZ$0 anchor for the basics ([Hello Club](https://helloclub.com/pricing); [Sporty pricing](https://sporty.co.nz/v2/pricing)). ShuttleOps, the closest badminton product, reportedly charges £4–12/month, though that figure is unofficial ([GitHub PR comment](https://github.com/mbstoker/Pegboard.Site/pull/22)). Volunteer clubs will compare you with Hello Club. Price by member band rather than court count, because badminton and table tennis clubs usually rent halls and own no courts.

| Tier (indicative, test with design partners) | Who it's for | Monthly price (NZD, excl. GST) |
|---|---|---|
| Club | Up to ~150 members: sessions, memberships, waitlist, rotation board, Arena, finance basics, subdomain | NZ$49–79 |
| Club Plus | Larger clubs: custom domain, more admins, full finance module and AGM report, leagues, association exports | NZ$99–149 |
| Venue (later) | Padel, pickleball and tennis venues: court grid, peak pricing, split payments, door access | NZ$249–399 |
| Native branded app (add-on) | Clubs that want their own App Store listing, built under their own developer account | ~NZ$500–900 setup + NZ$30–60/mo |

These bands come from the business-model research's analysis of comparable vendors and have not been tested with buyers ([business model notes](../research_notes/Racquet%20club%20software%20feature%20gaps/business_model_and_gtm.md)). Add a 15–20% annual-prepay discount, in line with CourtReserve's spread, and a 30-day free trial, which Hello Club treats as the norm.

On payments, use Stripe Connect direct charges with a small, transparent application fee (around 0.5–1%) plus a toggle to pass the fee to players. Spond lets clubs absorb or pass on its ~2.5% fee ([Klubraum](https://klubraum.com/blog/club-management-software/)), and Hello Club caps its POLi fee at NZ$1 for non-profits ([Hello Club help](https://help.helloclub.com/en/articles/2514965-account-to-account-payments-in-new-zealand)). By the researcher's rough estimate, a 150-member badminton club processes NZ$40–80k a year, so a 0.5–1% margin adds roughly **NZ$200–800 per club per year**. That is useful, but it is not a substitute for the subscription. Payments revenue matters far more with high-volume venues. Mindbody earned about 39% of its revenue from payments in its last public year ([Mindbody 10-K FY2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001458962/000145896218000003/mb_12-31x2017x10xk.htm)), which is why the venue tier is the long-term growth engine. Keep "no booking commission" as a headline promise. It is the counter-message every Playtomic and CourtReserve challenger uses ([Book & Go (competitor)](https://www.bookandgo.app/en/courtreserve-alternative)). Also keep a manual or offline payment path (bank transfer with reference codes, cash marked paid), because some treasurers will not use cards. The jersey campaign's 5-digit reference-code matching is a ready-made base for it ([lib/jersey.ts](../lib/jersey.ts)).

Be realistic about scale. NZ has perhaps 1,000–1,500 racquet clubs. At about NZ$1,000/year each, that is roughly **NZ$1–1.5M ARR at 100% market share**, which is the researcher's own estimate. NZ can support a bootstrapped business or a solid side income. A larger outcome needs Australia, where pickleball alone has **410–425 affiliated clubs and venues** and no national-body-funded club platform ([DUPR & Pickleball Australia](https://www.dupr.com/post/dupr-and-pickleball-australia)), and later the UK and US. Infrastructure is not the constraint: on a shared architecture, platform running costs fall from about US$15–20 per club per month at 10 clubs to US$1–4 at 1,000, provided SMS is not the default.

## Go to market through Canterbury, associations and coaches, in that order

The ClubSpark story shows the proven channel in community racquet sport: federation endorsement. The LTA gave it free to every registered UK venue and it reached **1,500–2,500+ UK organisations** ([LTA](https://www.lta.org.uk/news/2021/april/lta-launches-partnership-with-clubspark-to-provide-enhanced-online-booking-system/); [ClubSpark case study](https://clubspark.com/case-studies/delivering-british-tennis)). You cannot start there, but you can climb towards it.

Start with **Canterbury badminton**. Three Wolves is a live reference site that other clubs can watch on a real club night. Approach Badminton Canterbury and its clubs, and recruit 3–5 design partners on a founding-club discount, with free migration from spreadsheets, Hello Club or Sporty. Moving the member list and subscription status is the hardest switching cost for a volunteer committee. Timing matters. NZ badminton's main season runs roughly March to September, and committees decide before registrations open, so **October to February is the outreach window, and you are in it now**. Expect one or two committee meetings (one to three months) from first demo to decision; that is an inference, as no sourced cycle-length data exists.

Next, take a **regional association deal**. Associations already set affiliation rules: Badminton North Harbour requires at least 20 registered players, and Wellington North at least 12 ([BNH policy](https://www.bnh.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Registered-Clubs-Policy-1.pdf); [WNBA policy](https://www.wnba.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/20211016-affliated_club_policy-FINALv3.pdf)). An association-wide licence bundled with registration exports, inter-club events and levy collection is the ClubSpark playbook at NZ scale. In parallel, open a conversation with **Badminton NZ** about its national membership project. At best you become its platform. At worst you learn early that a rival has been chosen.

Then widen in two directions. Go to NZ table tennis clubs, where Table Tennis NZ's own IMR numbers and rankings mean you should integrate rather than replace ([TTNZ IMR](https://tabletennis.org.nz/how/apply-imr-number)). And go to social pickleball groups, many of which play in tennis clubs. Note that Pickleball NZ already endorses Hello Club for affiliated-club registration, so lead with the club-night tools, not registration ([Hello Club–PNZA](https://helloclub.com/blog/pnza-partners-with-hello-club)). Australian community pickleball clubs come after that. Commercial padel and pickleball venues wait for the venue tier.

Two cheap channels compound along the way. **Coaches** run sessions across several venues; a free or cheap coach tier, as the LTA gives accredited coaches, seeds the product into new clubs. **Players** belong to several clubs, so a shared player identity across clubs, with one login and one Arena profile, turns members into carriers. Keep the default app product-branded and multi-tenant for exactly this reason.

## Legal and compliance work is modest but must precede the first invoice

The core position is simple: **the club owns its member data and its money; the platform processes data on the club's behalf.** Publish SaaS terms that say so, with export on demand and on termination, a deletion window, liability capped at 12 months' fees, and notice of price changes. Publish a data processing agreement and a sub-processor list (Vercel, Neon, Stripe, Resend, Google/Firebase, Sanity). Under NZ's Privacy Act, **IPP3A took effect on 1 May 2026** and requires notice when personal information is collected indirectly. That matters whenever an association or club uploads a member list on members' behalf, and the club remains responsible for its agents ([Privacy Commissioner](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/ipp3a/); [Bell Gully](https://www.bellgully.com/insights/preparing-for-ipp-3a-new-requirements-effective-1-may-2026/)). Several sub-processors are likely US-hosted, so disclose them as overseas processors under the cross-border principle, and keep the database in Sydney, where it already sits.

Three more items need attention before launch. **Payments**: with Stripe direct charges the club is merchant of record and responsible for GST on its own fees. You charge GST on your SaaS fee and application fees. Clubs need a "GST-registered: yes/no" switch, and receipts above $200 must carry GST details on request ([PwC NZ](https://www.pwc.co.nz/services/tax/new-gst-invoicing-and-record-keeping-rules.html); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/saas-platforms-and-marketplaces)). Card data stays inside Stripe-hosted fields, which keeps your PCI burden light. **Australia**: many clubs are under the A$3m small-business privacy exemption, but its removal has been agreed in principle, so build to the stricter standard ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/de-de/wissen/publications/dc6752ce/does-the-privacy-act-apply-to-your-nfp-the-answer-might-surprise-you)). **Beyond ANZ**: the UK requires safeguarding officers, criminal record checks and coach accreditation tracking ([LTA venue standards](https://www.lta.org.uk/about-us/safeguarding/venue-standards/)), and US pickleball and padel expect digital waivers. Juniors need guardian-linked accounts everywhere.

Two housekeeping items are not in the research but are standard, and should be settled now. Get written confirmation of who owns the code (you, with Three Wolves holding a licence, or the club). And choose a neutral product brand: "Three Wolves" and the "Wolf" game vocabulary belong to one club, not to a product sold to other clubs. Take accounting and legal advice on the entity structure and the terms before the first paid contract.

## The main risks are channel lock-out, founder bandwidth and a data leak

The biggest commercial risk is **lock-out by national bodies**. Tennis is already decided (ClubSpark, with Sporty approved), Pickleball NZ has chosen Hello Club, and Badminton NZ could choose a vendor at any point. The mitigation is to talk to the bodies early and position Three Wolves as complementary club-night tooling that exports to whatever national system exists. The second risk is **low willingness to pay plus slow, committee-driven buying**. SMB SaaS loses roughly 2–5% of customers a month ([UserJot](https://userjot.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks)), so retention levers (finance history, player network, payments) matter as much as acquisition. The third is **capacity**: a solo founder doing 4–7 months of platform work while also selling, migrating clubs and doing support will stall unless the scope stays as narrow as the roadmap above.

Technical risks are concentrated rather than diffuse. A **cross-club data leak** through a missing `club_id` filter or a shared page cache would be fatal to trust. Use row-level security, a database role that cannot bypass it, tenant-keyed caching, and an automated isolation test in CI that tries to read club B's data as club A ([Neon on RLS](https://neon.com/blog/is-postgres-rls-for-everything-and-everyone)). **Notification failures** destroy trust in session apps, as Spond's reviews show, so the waitlist and push work deserves real testing. **WhatsApp inertia** is real: a Singapore badminton group's purpose-built sign-up app never displaced its WhatsApp list ([unplugged-badminton](https://github.com/gjsjessy/unplugged-badminton)). Keep joining by link or QR, without an account, and post updates back into the group chat. The existing tests are unit tests of pure logic only, with no integration or end-to-end tests, and preview deployments share the production database, so add end-to-end tests before other clubs' money flows through the system ([tests/](../tests/)).

The evidence itself is a risk. Every competitor price in this report comes from search summaries or third-party listings because vendor sites were blocked. Some sources conflict outright: one widely read review appears to attach PlayByPoint's tier names to CourtReserve ([Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/pickleball-gear/best-pickleball-court-reservation-software)). No independent club reviews or Reddit threads were retrievable, and Hello Club's feature list could not be inspected. Treat the comparison table as a hypothesis to confirm in demos, not as settled fact.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the commercial question. Three Wolves does not mainly lack features. It is a single-club application whose best ideas (fair rotation, an engagement game built on attendance, treasurer-grade NZ finance) happen to be what volunteer racquet clubs struggle with most and what incumbents have stopped building for them. The work that unlocks revenue is mostly invisible to players: a tenant model, configuration, roles and Connect payments. The features worth adding before launch are the boring ones that remove admin (auto-promotion, packs, refunds, push), not more gamification.

Strategically, the product's strongest long-term asset may be the player network rather than any single club. A shared player identity and Arena profile across clubs gives Three Wolves a cross-club network that per-club white-label apps cannot build and Hello Club does not appear to offer, and it could grow into the social badminton rating the sport lacks. Do not build that before the first paying club, but do not design it out either. The window that matters right now is calendar-driven: committees decide over the NZ summer, so the next three months should go to design-partner commitments and platform foundations, not to the venue tier or native apps.
