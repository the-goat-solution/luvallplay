## What Vision does

You set a phone on a tripod behind the court. Vision watches the game and works out what happened: it finds the players, follows them, picks up each hit and measures how far and how fast everyone moved.

Afterwards you get the numbers for your game, such as:

- Hits
- Rallies, and your longest rally
- Distance covered
- Top speed
- Fastest shot

## Who can use it

Two honest limits:

- **Badminton only.** Vision is trained on badminton. Other sports may follow, but we're not promising dates.
- **Members of the first club on Luvallplay only, for now.** There it's used for Arena challenges.

A match needs a pass. Members can see the current pass details in the app before they start.

## What you need

- **A recent phone.** For live tracking, the club asks for an iPhone 14 or newer.
- **A tripod or a secure mount.** The phone has to stay completely still.
- **Space behind the court.** A few steps back from the baseline.

## How to film

### 1. Put the phone behind the court

Stand behind one baseline, in the middle, and look down the length of the court. That's the view Vision needs. Don't film from the side.

### 2. Get some height

Raise the tripod so the phone looks slightly down at the court. Around head height or higher works well. If a fence or a ledge is in the right spot, a secure mount there is fine.

### 3. Fit the whole court in the frame

You should see all 4 corners, both back lines and the net. If you can't get far enough back, switch to your phone's 0.5× ultra-wide lens. It fits the whole court from closer.

### 4. Keep it steady

No hands. A phone that shakes, slides or gets bumped gives poor results. Tighten the tripod and keep bags and feet away from its legs.

### 5. Mark the court

Vision asks you to tap the court's corners on the screen, so it knows where the lines are. If a corner is out of shot, pick another point it offers, such as where the service lines meet.

### 6. Say where everyone starts

Tell Vision which player is standing where. That's how it knows who is who, even if you're all in the same club shirt.

### 7. Play

Start the game and forget the phone is there.

## Two ways to use it

- **Live.** The phone tracks the game as you play.
- **From a video.** Film the game first, then run the same tracking on the recording.

## Tips for better results

- **Check the light.** A bright hall works best. Avoid pointing the phone at a window or a strong light.
- **Clear the background.** People walking behind the court can confuse things. Pick a quieter court if you can.
- **Do a 10-second test.** Check the framing before the first serve, not after the last one.

## Ask before you film

A camera on a court films everyone on it, and sometimes the people on the next court.

- Tell the other 3 players before you start. If someone would rather not be filmed, don't film.
- Point the phone at your court only.
- Follow your club's and your venue's rules about filming.
- Don't film juniors without their guardian's agreement.

## Where your video goes

At the first club, the video stays on your phone. Only the final numbers are saved, and only for challenges where a phone was set up.

## The numbers are a guide

Vision is good, not perfect. Poor light, a bumped phone or 4 people in identical shirts can throw it off. Use the stats to spot patterns over several games, not to settle an argument about one rally.
